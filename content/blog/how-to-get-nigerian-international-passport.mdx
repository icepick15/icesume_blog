Every scholarship, remote job offer requiring travel, or Japa plan eventually runs into the same requirement: a valid international passport. Yet the application process — and its real timelines — trips up many first-time applicants.

This guide walks through the current process for both first-time applicants and renewals, managed by the **Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS)**.

---

## Step 1: Determine Which Passport Type You Need

Nigeria issues a 32-page and a 64-page international passport, each available in **5-year** and **10-year** validity (10-year validity applies to adults only). Choose based on:

- **How often you travel:** frequent travellers benefit from the 64-page booklet to avoid running out of visa pages
- **Your age:** minors are only issued 5-year validity passports regardless of page count

---

## Step 2: Register and Book an Appointment Online

All applications start on the **Nigeria Immigration Service's online portal** (immigration.gov.ng). Walk-in applications without prior online registration are generally not accepted at most passport offices.

1. Create an account on the NIS portal
2. Select passport type (new/renewal, 32 or 64 pages, 5 or 10 years)
3. Fill in your personal details and upload required documents
4. Pay the application fee online
5. Book an appointment slot at your preferred passport office

**Tip:** Appointment slots at major city offices (Lagos, Abuja) can be limited — check regularly for openings, including early morning when new slots sometimes release.

---

## Step 3: Required Documents

Have these ready before you start the online application, as scanned copies are required for upload:

- **Valid means of identification** (National ID/NIN slip, voter's card, or driver's licence)
- **Birth certificate or age declaration**
- **Passport photograph** meeting NIS specifications (white background, specific dimensions)
- **Previous passport** (for renewals)
- **Proof of address** (utility bill or similar, in some cases)

For minors, additional documents (parental consent, guardian ID) are required.

---

## Step 4: Pay the Application Fee

Fees vary by passport type and validity — always confirm the current fee directly on the NIS portal before paying, since fees are periodically reviewed. Payment is made online through the portal's integrated payment system; avoid any agent asking for cash payment outside the official portal.

---

## Step 5: Attend Your Appointment

At your scheduled appointment:
- Bring **original copies** of every document you uploaded online
- Biometric capture (fingerprints, photograph, signature) is done in person
- Verification of your submitted documents against the originals

Arrive early — passport offices in major cities can have significant volume even with the appointment system.

---

## Step 6: Track and Collect Your Passport

Processing time varies, but plan for **several weeks** from appointment to collection under normal conditions — apply well ahead of any known travel date (scholarship deadlines, visa interview dates, flight bookings) rather than close to it. You can track application status through the NIS portal.

---

## If You're Applying From Abroad

Nigerians resident outside Nigeria apply through the Nigerian **embassy, high commission, or consulate** in their country of residence, not the domestic NIS portal. Processing steps are broadly similar but managed through the specific mission — check that mission's website for their exact process and fees, as these can differ from the domestic process.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Applying too close to a travel deadline.** Passport processing delays are common enough that applying with only weeks before a scholarship or visa deadline is risky.
- **Using an agent who asks for payment outside the official NIS portal.** This bypasses the legitimate system and carries real risk of fraud.
- **Submitting a photo that doesn't meet specifications** — a common cause of application delays.
- **Not double-checking name spelling and date of birth against your birth certificate/NIN** before submission — mismatches cause processing issues that are frustrating to correct later.
- **Assuming all passport offices have the same appointment availability** — some offices outside major cities may have shorter wait times than Lagos or Abuja.
