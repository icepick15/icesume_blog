Getting invited for an interview means your CV already did its job. What happens next depends entirely on preparation — and most candidates walk in having only thought about their CV, not the actual conversation ahead.

This guide covers the interview formats you'll encounter in Nigeria, the questions that come up again and again, and how to answer them without sounding rehearsed.

---

## The Three Interview Formats to Expect

**1. Phone/screening interview** — usually with HR or a recruiter, 15-30 minutes, focused on basic fit and salary expectations.

**2. Panel interview** — common in banks, multinationals, and larger Nigerian companies. Multiple interviewers, more structured, often includes both technical and behavioural questions.

**3. Technical/practical assessment** — a task, case study, or skills test, especially common for tech, finance, and analytical roles.

Ask the recruiter directly what format to expect if it isn't stated — this alone puts you ahead of candidates who walk in blind.

## The Questions That Come Up in Almost Every Nigerian Interview

### "Tell me about yourself"
This is not an invitation for your life story. Keep it to a 60-90 second summary: your current role/situation, your most relevant experience, and why you're interested in this specific role.

### "Why do you want to work here?"
Weak answer: "I need a job" (even if true, never say this). Strong answer: reference something specific about the company — their market position, a product, their growth — that shows you researched them, not just applied everywhere.

### "What is your salary expectation?"
Don't answer with a single fixed number too early if you can avoid it. A stronger approach: give a realistic range based on market research, and note it's negotiable based on the full offer. For a deeper strategy on this specific question, see our guide on [how to negotiate your salary in Nigeria](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-in-nigeria).

### "Why did you leave your last job?" / "Why are you job searching?"
Never speak negatively about a previous employer, no matter how justified. Frame it around growth: seeking more responsibility, a better fit for your skills, or a new challenge.

### "What are your weaknesses?"
Avoid the cliché fake-weakness answer ("I work too hard"). Give a real but manageable weakness, and show what you're actively doing about it.

### Behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time when...")
These test how you actually behave under real conditions — conflict, pressure, failure. Use the **STAR method**: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Prepare 3-4 real stories from your work history in advance so you're not building an answer live.

## Questions You Should Ask Them

Never say "no questions" when asked if you have any — it signals disengagement. Strong questions to ask:

- "What does success look like in this role in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the team structure I'd be working within?"
- "What's the next step in the process, and what's the expected timeline?"

Avoid leading with questions about salary, leave days, or remote-work policy in a first interview — save those for when an offer is on the table or the interviewer raises them first.

---

## Preparing for Panel and Multinational Interviews

- **Research the company's actual products/services**, recent news, and their position in their industry — not just their "About Us" page.
- **Prepare for at least one competency-based question per core skill** listed in the job description.
- **Dress and present professionally** even for virtual interviews — test your camera, lighting, and internet connection beforehand.
- **Bring printed copies of your CV** to in-person panel interviews, even if you already submitted it online.

---

## After the Interview

- **Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours**, referencing something specific discussed in the interview — this is uncommon in Nigeria and quietly sets candidates apart.
- **Follow up if you haven't heard back** by the timeline they gave you, with a polite, brief check-in — not multiple messages in quick succession.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Arriving late** — for in-person interviews in Lagos especially, always plan for traffic and aim to arrive 15-20 minutes early.
- **Not researching the company beyond the job title.**
- **Giving vague answers with no specific examples** — interviewers remember specifics, not general claims like "I'm a hard worker."
- **Speaking negatively about past employers** or colleagues.
- **Not preparing questions to ask** — it's one of the easiest things to prepare for and one of the most commonly skipped.
