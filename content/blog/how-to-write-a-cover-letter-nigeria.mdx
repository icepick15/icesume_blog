Many Nigerian job seekers treat the cover letter as an afterthought — a generic paragraph pasted into every application, or skipped entirely when it isn't explicitly required. That's a mistake: a genuinely tailored cover letter is one of the few places you can directly address why *you*, specifically, fit *this* role, before a recruiter even opens your CV.

This guide covers how to structure one that actually gets read, paired with our guide on [how to write a Nigerian CV that gets interviews](/blog/how-to-write-a-nigerian-cv-that-gets-interviews).

---

## When You Actually Need One

- **Always include one when the application explicitly requests it** — skipping it here can get you filtered out automatically.
- **Include a short one even when optional**, for roles you're genuinely targeting — it's a low-cost way to stand out.
- **You can skip it** for quick, high-volume applications to roles you're only moderately interested in, where CV alone is the norm (e.g. some job board "quick apply" listings).

---

## The Structure That Actually Works

### Opening (1-2 sentences)
State the specific role you're applying for and one strong, relevant hook — not "I am writing to apply for..." Instead: name a specific achievement or connection to the company immediately.

**Weak:** "I am writing to apply for the Marketing Executive position advertised on your website."

**Stronger:** "Having grown a small business's social following from 2,000 to 40,000 followers in eight months, I'm applying for the Marketing Executive role to bring that same growth-focused approach to [Company]."

### Middle (2-3 short paragraphs)
Connect **specific** parts of your experience directly to **specific** requirements in the job description. Don't repeat your CV line by line — instead, expand on one or two achievements most relevant to this exact role, with real numbers where possible (revenue impact, efficiency gained, targets hit).

### Closing (1-2 sentences)
Restate your interest specifically in this company/role, and include a clear, confident call to action (e.g., availability for an interview).

## Example Short Cover Letter

> Dear [Hiring Manager's Name],
>
> Having grown a small business's social following from 2,000 to 40,000 followers in eight months while managing a ₦50,000 monthly ad budget, I'm applying for the Marketing Executive role at [Company] to bring that same growth-focused, budget-conscious approach to your team.
>
> In my current role at [Current Company], I led our first paid social campaign, which brought in 120 qualified leads in six weeks at a 30% lower cost-per-lead than our previous agency-run campaigns. I'm particularly drawn to [Company]'s recent expansion into [specific area], and believe my experience scaling campaigns on a limited budget is directly relevant to that growth stage.
>
> I'd welcome the opportunity to discuss how I can contribute to your marketing team. I'm available for an interview at your convenience.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

Notice: specific numbers, a specific reference to the company, and no generic filler sentences.

## Finding the Hiring Manager's Name

Never default to "To Whom It May Concern" if it can be avoided. Check:
- The job posting itself (sometimes names a contact)
- The company's LinkedIn page — search for people with "HR," "Talent," or "Recruiting" in their title
- The company website's team/leadership page

If you genuinely cannot find a name after reasonable effort, "Dear Hiring Manager" is an acceptable fallback — it's still better than "To Whom It May Concern."

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Reusing the exact same cover letter for every application.** Recruiters can tell, and generic letters are usually skimmed and discarded.
- **Repeating your CV instead of expanding on it.** The cover letter's job is to add context and narrative, not duplicate bullet points.
- **Making it too long.** Three to four short paragraphs is enough — a cover letter longer than one page rarely gets fully read.
- **Focusing on what you want from the job rather than what you bring to it.** Frame everything around value to the employer, not personal need.
- **Typos and copy-paste errors** — especially leaving a previous company's name in a letter meant for a different application. Always proofread before sending.
