Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) is the tax deducted directly from your salary every month before it hits your account. Almost every salaried Nigerian has seen the line on their payslip — far fewer understand how the number is actually calculated, or that the rules changed significantly starting **1 January 2026** under the new **Nigeria Tax Act (NTA) 2025**.

This guide breaks down how PAYE works now, what changed, and how to estimate your own take-home pay.

---

## What Is PAYE and Who Pays It?

PAYE is Nigeria's system for collecting personal income tax from employees. Instead of you filing and paying tax yourself, your **employer calculates, deducts, and remits it** to the relevant state's Internal Revenue Service (or FIRS for certain categories) on your behalf, every month.

It applies to anyone earning employment income in Nigeria — salary, wages, allowances, bonuses, and most benefits-in-kind.

---

## What Changed in 2026

The **Nigeria Tax Act (NTA) 2025**, effective from 1 January 2026, restructured how chargeable income and tax bands work, aiming to reduce the burden on lower earners. The two headline changes:

1. **A much higher tax-free threshold.** The first **₦800,000** of your annual chargeable income is now taxed at **0%** — a major shift from the previous system where tax applied from far lower income levels.
2. **Revised tax bands** above that threshold, generally rising in steps as income increases.

**Note:** Because this is a newly implemented reform, some published sources cite slightly different band breakdowns as FIRS finalises implementation guidance. Always cross-check the current bands on [firs.gov.ng](https://www.firs.gov.ng) or with your employer's payroll/HR team before making financial decisions based on exact figures.

The commonly cited 2026 band structure is:

| Chargeable Income Band | Rate |
|---|---|
| First ₦800,000 | 0% |
| Next ₦2,200,000 (₦800k–₦3m) | 15% |
| Next ₦9,000,000 (₦3m–₦12m) | 18% |
| Next ₦13,000,000 (₦12m–₦25m) | 21% |
| Next ₦25,000,000 (₦25m–₦50m) | 23% |
| Above ₦50,000,000 | 25% |

## PAYE Is Calculated on "Chargeable Income," Not Your Gross Salary

This is the part most people get wrong. Your tax band applies to your **chargeable income** — gross income minus allowable deductions and reliefs — not your raw salary.

**Allowable deductions before tax is calculated include:**
- **Pension contributions** — your mandatory 8% employee contribution to your Retirement Savings Account (see our guide on [understanding your pension in Nigeria](/blog/understanding-pension-in-nigeria))
- **National Housing Fund (NHF)** contributions — 2.5% of basic salary, where applicable
- **Life assurance premiums** — up to ₦100,000
- **Rent relief** — 20% of gross income, capped at ₦500,000, for employees who pay rent

**A simplified example:**

If your gross annual salary is ₦4,800,000:
1. Subtract your 8% pension contribution (₦384,000) and other applicable reliefs
2. Apply rent relief if eligible (up to ₦500,000)
3. What's left is your chargeable income — the amount the tax bands above actually apply to

This is why two people with the same gross salary can have noticeably different take-home pay — pension contributions, rent relief, and other reliefs change what's actually taxed.

## How to Check Your Employer Is Deducting Correctly

1. **Get your payslip breakdown**, not just the final number. It should show gross pay, pension deduction, NHF (if applicable), taxable income, and the PAYE amount.
2. **Confirm your pension and NHF are actually being remitted** — deductions on paper mean nothing if they're not paid into your RSA or NHF account.
3. **Recalculate manually once a year**, especially after a salary increase, bonus, or change in personal circumstances (e.g. starting to pay rent that qualifies for relief).
4. **Ask HR/Payroll directly** if a deduction looks off — under-deduction becomes your liability later; over-deduction is money you're owed back.

---

## Common PAYE Mistakes and Misunderstandings

- **Assuming PAYE is calculated on gross salary.** It's calculated on chargeable income after reliefs — ignoring this makes people think they're overtaxed (or undertaxed) when the math is actually correct.
- **Not claiming rent relief** because they don't know it exists or don't submit proof of tenancy to HR.
- **Confusing PAYE with the old tax bands** — if you're using an old calculator or spreadsheet from before 2026, your numbers will be wrong under the new Nigeria Tax Act structure.
- **Freelancers and business owners assuming PAYE rules apply to them directly** — self-employed income is taxed differently (direct assessment), not through employer PAYE.
