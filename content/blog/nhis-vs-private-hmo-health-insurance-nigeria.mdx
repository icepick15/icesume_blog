Healthcare costs are one of the most common financial shocks Nigerian households face — a single hospital admission can wipe out months of savings. Yet most Nigerians either have no health coverage at all, or have employer-provided HMO coverage they've never actually reviewed.

This guide breaks down how Nigeria's public and private health insurance options work, and how to think about your own coverage.

---

## The Public Option: NHIS / NHIA

Nigeria's national health insurance scheme has gone through a rebrand and reform — it now operates under the **National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA)**, established by the National Health Insurance Authority Act 2022, which replaced the older National Health Insurance Scheme (NHIS) framework.

**Key things to know:**
- Coverage is mandatory in principle for all Nigerians under the reformed law, though rollout and enforcement are still expanding across states
- Formal sector employees are typically enrolled through their employer
- Informal sector workers, students, and others can enrol through state health insurance schemes (many states now run their own agencies aligned with NHIA)
- Coverage typically includes basic consultations, a defined drug list, and specified procedures — but often with lower limits and a narrower hospital network than private HMO plans

**Where to check your state's scheme:** most states now have a state health insurance agency (e.g. Lagos State Health Scheme) — search for your specific state's health insurance agency for enrolment details, since implementation varies by state.

---

## The Private Option: HMOs

**Health Maintenance Organisations (HMOs)** are private companies licensed to manage health coverage, typically accessed either through an employer-sponsored plan or purchased individually.

**How it typically works:**
1. You (or your employer) pay a premium, usually annually
2. You're assigned or choose a primary healthcare provider ("gatekeeper" hospital/clinic)
3. Routine care goes through that provider; referrals are needed for specialists or bigger hospitals
4. Coverage tiers vary significantly — higher-tier plans cover more hospitals, higher claim limits, and sometimes dental/optical extras

**Well-known HMOs operating in Nigeria include:** Hygeia HMO, AXA Mansard Health, Reliance HMO, Avon HMO, and several others regulated by NHIA.

---

## If You Have Employer-Provided HMO Coverage — Actually Check It

Most employees never review their HMO plan details until they need to use it — often at the worst possible time. Do this now, not during an emergency:

1. **Find out which HMO your employer uses** and your plan tier (HR can provide this).
2. **Check your covered hospital list** — confirm at least one hospital near where you actually live is on it, not just near the office.
3. **Check your annual claim limit** and what's excluded (many plans exclude or cap maternity, dental, optical, and certain chronic conditions separately).
4. **Save your HMO's emergency/authorization contact number** in your phone before you need it.

## Should You Get Additional Coverage Beyond What's Provided?

Consider supplementing your coverage if:
- Your employer-provided plan has a low annual limit relative to private hospital costs in your city
- You have dependents (spouse, children, parents) not automatically covered under your work plan
- You have a pre-existing condition with limited coverage under your current plan
- You're self-employed or in the informal sector with no coverage at all

**Options if you need to buy coverage independently:**
- Individual HMO plans (most major HMOs offer these directly, not just through employers)
- State health insurance scheme enrolment, often significantly cheaper than private HMO premiums
- A dedicated health emergency fund as a minimum baseline if formal coverage isn't immediately affordable

---

## What Health Insurance in Nigeria Typically Does NOT Cover Well

Be realistic about limits, regardless of which option you choose:
- Major surgeries and long-term chronic disease management often exceed annual claim limits on lower-tier plans
- Treatment abroad is essentially never covered by domestic NHIS/HMO plans
- Some plans exclude pre-existing conditions for an initial waiting period after enrolment

This is why financial advisors often recommend a health emergency fund **in addition to** insurance, not instead of it — insurance covers routine and moderate costs; savings cover the gap for anything larger.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Not knowing your own coverage details** until you need care and discover a hospital isn't on your plan.
- **Assuming employer coverage extends to family** — many plans only cover the employee unless dependents are explicitly added (often at extra cost).
- **Ignoring state health insurance options** because they're less marketed than private HMOs, even though they can be significantly cheaper for informal-sector workers.
- **Having zero emergency health fund** on the assumption that insurance alone will cover any situation.
